# JAID AI Philosophy
## Joining Ancestors In Dialogue

### Why we built JAID
JAID was built in honor of the people who introduced me to CHamoru culture and language — my ex, who first opened that world to me, and my friend, who taught me words and shared his heritage with generosity. It was also built with urgency. CHamoru is a critically endangered language. JAID exists to help perpetuate it before it is too late — to give diaspora learners a way back to something that belongs to them.

### The problem with AI and endangered languages
AI can scale things humans cannot. But AI can also get things wrong with complete confidence. For a language spoken by few people, confident errors are dangerous. A wrong answer about Spanish will be corrected by millions of speakers. A wrong Chamorro word or pronunciation, reinforced across a diaspora, could accelerate the very loss we are trying to prevent. The stakes are not equal and we do not treat them as equal.

### What AI does in JAID
AI in JAID operates only where it cannot cause linguistic harm:

- The hint system gives learners contextual clues about meaning and cultural significance — Claude never generates or evaluates Chamorro language content
- The study suggestion system reads a learner's progress and offers personalized coaching — no language content is produced
- When cultural context is discussed, it is grounded in content I have sourced from the Topping, Ogo, and Dungca Chamorro Dictionary and validated by a native speaker — Claude clarifies and coaches around that content, not beyond it

### What AI does NOT do in JAID — and why
- **Grading Chamorro responses** — Claude cannot reliably evaluate Chamorro pronunciation or spelling. Incorrect feedback would teach learners the wrong thing.
- **Generating Chamorro sentences or vocabulary** — Claude's Chamorro training data is insufficient and potentially inaccurate. We do not know what it learned or from where.
- **Suggesting new vocabulary** — only Topping-sourced, native-speaker-validated content appears to learners. No exceptions.
- **Pronunciation feedback** — Chamorro requires native speaker audio. Text-to-speech does not support Chamorro and would produce confidently wrong pronunciations.

### Our content standard
Every word and saying in JAID must appear in the Topping, Ogo, and Dungca Chamorro Reference Grammar and Dictionary AND be validated by our native speaker collaborator. One source alone is not enough. Both are required.

### The rule
**AI coaches the learner. AI never teaches the language.**

### Who validates JAID's content
JAID's content is validated by a close friend who is a fluent native CHamoru speaker, a dancer of traditional CHamoru dance, and a proud son of Guahån. He collaborates with me on every word — validating spelling, meaning, sentence structure, and cultural significance before anything appears in the app. He is under 29 years old. Fluent native CHamoru speakers of his generation are rare. That rarity is exactly why JAID exists.
